## Tutorial

The tutorial runs through chapter 4. If you just follow the prompts you won't get stuck. By the end, your queen ant should be around level 8.

## Priority #1: Level up your queen ant

The Ants is heavily a "growth" game, and your queen's level caps everything else. Your anthill level equals your queen's level, and almost every building is capped by it — even buildings bought from the store.

### How long it actually takes (three players' records)

General guides are vaguer than the numbers people who actually ran it wrote down. Three Japanese players documented reaching a target queen level for a rewards campaign:

| Player | Level reached | Days | Spending | Raided by other players? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net de Kasegu Point Seikatsu | Lv18 | 10 | Free-to-play | Never once |
| Game to Poikatsu +α | Lv19 | 14 | Free-to-play (used diamonds) | Raided, but lost no resources |
| Sontaku Nashi no Poikatsu Blog | Lv19 | 20 | Free-to-play | Raided |

All three were free-to-play and reached level 18–19 in 10–20 days. One recorded the pace day by day: level 7 on day 1, 9 on day 3, 13 on day 6, 15 on day 10, 17 on day 16, and 19 on day 20. **Progress is fast early and slows sharply after around level 15** — the numbers show it clearly.

### Should you push to level 25? (opinions differ)

English-language guides usually say getting the queen to level 25 makes the rest of the game noticeably easier, and this page used to say the same. Japanese players' actual play records rate it differently: one concluded that **level 18 is the sweet spot, and that from level 22 on, the resources and time required grow out of proportion to what you get back.**

The answer depends on whether you are optimising for a short sprint or a long-term account. "Rush to 25" is not the only correct answer.

## Where everyone gets stuck

All three records agree on one thing: **what runs out is resources, not build slots.** And it is the same resources every time.

- **Honeydew runs dry first.** It cannot be produced through quick production, so you cannot bulk it up in a hurry the way you can with other resources. Around level 19 one player found everything except fungus and honeydew was manageable — honeydew alone was the blocker
- **Plants run short around level 17.** Upgrading the carrier ant hatchery eats a lot of plants, and silkworm feeding consumes plants at the same time, so the two compete
- The countermeasures they listed: secure honeydew above everything else, take the Cultivation trait, and push "rapid production research" early

The player who hit level 18 in 10 days says they had all three in mind from the start. The one who took 20 days looked back and said they had "wasted quite a lot of effort."

## What actually happens when your shield expires

At queen level 8 the new-player shield expires automatically and you become raidable. This is the scariest moment in the early game, but the records suggest **the real damage is limited**.

- The player who reached level 18 in 10 days was **never raided once**, and credits being in a strong alliance
- Players who were raided **lost nothing as long as their resources were under the warehouse protection cap**. One also received relief supplies from the developers after their first raid, so the game seems designed not to break new players
- If you want to avoid losing troops, one player recommends **turning off participation in defensive garrisons**

So the thing to actually guard against early on is not being raided — it is **sitting on resources above your protection cap**.

## Playstyle trait system

Unlocked at queen level 7, "playstyle traits" let you pick a colony trait from three lines: Cultivation, Plunder, or Pastoral. Each has a dedicated building and exactly 3 clear pros and 3 clear cons (currently switchable for free).

| Trait | Dedicated building | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cultivation | Resource Processing Plant | Resource protection (harder to raid) / +50% gathering speed / +100% troop carrying capacity while gathering | -30% ATK while invading / -30% DEF while invading / -30% march speed |
| Plunder | Loot Warehouse | +150% troop carrying capacity while invading / 40% of would-be deaths convert to wounded / +100% wounded-troop healing speed | -30% plant/meat/wet-soil/sand output / -30% honeydew output / -50% troop carrying capacity while gathering |
| Pastoral | Resource Transport Passage | +50% honeydew output / -30% troop food consumption / -5% prices at the Traveling Insect Habitat | +100% resources lost to raids while invaded / -50% wounded-troop healing speed (a possible 3rd con unconfirmed) |

*My own account's playstyle-trait selection screen (measured 2026-07-19; Cultivation and Plunder rows measured that day, Pastoral row confirmed 2026-07-26)*

Every one of the three records above chose **Cultivation**, because resource protection and gathering speed hit the early-game bottleneck — resources — directly.

## Rerolling and spending

Rerolling is generally not recommended: the process is tedious, and normal progression already gives you strong ants fairly easily.

On spending, all three records above reached level 18–19 **free-to-play**. One used diamonds, but only diamonds earned in game. Daily login bonuses and daily tasks are generous enough that free-to-play progresses fine.

## Joining an alliance

Joining an alliance clearly speeds up your nest's development — and, as the records show, **it also affects whether you get raided at all**. The player who hit level 18 in 10 days credits a strong alliance for never being raided once. Join early. See [Alliance & PvP](alliance-pvp.html) for more.

## Common early mistakes to avoid

- Stockpiling resources above the "protected" threshold, making yourself a raid target
- Running hatchery upgrades without planning for the honeydew and plant shortages that follow
- Skipping recurring events (dailies, alliance expeditions) and missing out on steady rewards
